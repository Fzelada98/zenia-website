AI Agent for Home Care Agencies: The 2026 US Playbook

Short answer: non-medical home care in the US is a $173-192B category growing at roughly 4-7% a year (IBISWorld 2026), and the bottleneck is no longer demand. It is intake. 91% of agency leaders surveyed by AxisCare are already using or planning to use AI, and the family member usually calls at 8pm on a Sunday from the hospital parking lot. An AI agent that answers in under 60 seconds, qualifies hours, zip code and payer, and books a consult straight into WellSky Personal Care or AlayaCare is the single highest-leverage change a non-medical agency can make in 2026. Inside: 7 jobs the agent actually does, a 42-caregiver Phoenix 90-day case, the US stack (WellSky, AlayaCare, AxisCare, HHAeXchange, CareSmartz360 + Vapi/Retell + WhatsApp BSP + 10DLC + Stripe), a refuse-list that keeps you clear of clinical advice and TCPA trouble, 2026 pricing bands and a 2-week rollout.

Why home care intake is the leak, not demand

The macro picture is already clean. IBISWorld puts US home care providers at roughly $173.6-191.6 billion in 2026 depending on the edition, with a five-year CAGR between 4.6% and 6.7%. ShiftCare's 2026 private duty report says more than 500,000 caregiver positions sit unfilled nationally and that non-medical personal care is the core volume driver for most agencies, with private-pay rates in the $25-$35/hour band. Nearly 59% of agencies told the same tracker they were workforce-constrained in 2025.

What those numbers mean operationally: the growth is there. What breaks is the intake call. The Invoca 2026 Senior Care Lead Conversion Benchmarks Report and the June 2026 HCAOA newsletter both land on the same point: speed-to-lead is the single highest-leverage variable in home care sales, and AxisCare research cited in that newsletter found 91% of home care agency leaders are using or planning to use AI.

Three uncomfortable facts behind the data:

  • The caller is rarely the client. Non-medical intake calls come from the adult child, the hospital discharge planner, or the geriatric care manager. They have minutes, not hours. If voicemail picks up, they call the next agency on the list.
  • The call does not come at 11am. Industry call-pattern data consistently shows 50-65% of first-contact inquiries arrive outside 8am-5pm Monday-Friday: evenings, weekends, and the hour after a hospital discharge meeting.
  • Baseline conversion is low. Vendor-published figures suggest the average agency converts about one-third of inquiries into started clients, with wide variance. Web forms on senior care sites typically convert at 2-5%. Those are vendor claims, but every agency owner we talk to recognizes the shape.

None of that gets fixed by hiring another intake coordinator. It gets fixed by putting an AI agent in front of the phone, SMS, WhatsApp and web chat, with a hard handoff to a human when the lead is real.

What an AI agent actually does for a home care agency (7 jobs)

"AI agent" here means one assistant that lives across voice, SMS, WhatsApp and website chat, writes back to the scheduling and CRM system, and hands off to a live intake coordinator at a defined point. Not a tree-based FAQ widget. Not an answering service.

1. First-ring pickup, 24/7

The agent picks up every inbound call in under 3 seconds, greets by agency name, and takes the caller through a short qualification script. Nights, weekends, holidays, and the gap between the intake coordinator's calls. Target: live-answer rate from the 45-70% range typical of understaffed agencies to 95%+.

2. 8-field qualification without sounding like a form

The agent captures: client name and age, caller relationship, zip code (service area check), hours needed per week, primary ADL needs (bathing, transfer, meals, medication reminders, companionship), payer source (private pay, LTC insurance, VA Aid & Attendance, Medicaid waiver, hospice complement), start-date urgency, and preferred language of caregiver. Everything goes into the CRM as a structured record.

3. Live consult booking into the real calendar

When the lead qualifies, the agent books a free in-home or virtual consult directly on the agency's calendar, with drive-time and intake coordinator availability respected. No "we will call you back." A link with the time is sent by SMS and WhatsApp with a reschedule option.

4. 30/60/90-day dormant family reactivation

The agent tracks every inquiry that did not start services and reaches out at day 30 (status check), day 60 (service-level change?) and day 90 (any change in situation?). In a 42-caregiver agency this one workflow typically surfaces 8-14 started clients a quarter that would otherwise be lost.

5. Caregiver recruiting intake

Non-medical agencies lose as many leads on the recruiting side as the client side. Caregiver turnover sits at a reported 40-60% annually. The same agent fields CNA, HHA and companion applicants: name, state certification, availability, zip code, reliable transportation, English level. Routes to the recruiter with the shift gap matched.

6. Reminders and client-family status loops

Twenty-four-hour, two-hour and thirty-minute shift-start reminders to the caregiver and the family. Post-shift "everything OK?" check-in to the responsible party on day one, week one, month one. Any flagged reply routes to the care manager.

7. Out-of-scope handoff with a clean record

Clinical questions, medication dosing, hospital-level needs, Medicare Part A coverage questions, and urgent fall or injury reports go straight to the care manager with a structured summary. The agent never pretends to answer them. (More on the refuse-list below.)

The AI agent layer

  • Voice: Vapi or Retell as the voice stack, with ElevenLabs or Cartesia for the voice, Claude Haiku or GPT-4o-mini for the intake brain. Latency budget under 700 ms for turn-taking so the caller does not hang up.
  • WhatsApp: Meta Cloud API via a Business Solution Provider (360dialog, Gupshup or Twilio's WhatsApp product). Templates for the appointment reminder, the dormant reactivation and the caregiver application acknowledgement. One-time template approval per language.
  • SMS: 10DLC-registered number through Twilio or Telnyx. Brand registration first, campaign second. Expect 1-3 weeks for a clean registration in late 2026 under current Campaign Registry rules.
  • Web chat: Embedded on the agency site with the same prompt and the same writeback. The handoff model should degrade gracefully to SMS if the visitor closes the tab.
  • Payments: Stripe or Square for private-pay deposits, with a HIPAA-adjacent pay link rather than card numbers over text.

Alternatives if you are not building custom

Dialzara, Sameday, MyAIFrontDesk and Thoughtly all have home care or senior care playbooks. They work. They are shallower on CRM writeback than a custom build and will not reactivate dormant families without manual exports. Price band for SaaS: $249-$799/month per location plus setup. Price band for a built-for-you agent with real WellSky or AlayaCare writeback: $797-$1,497/month plus a one-time build of $2,400-$7,400 depending on integrations. Rough but close.

Compliance: the four rails that keep an agency safe

1. BAA coverage and scope

Even non-medical agencies collect protected health information the moment a family describes an ADL need, a diagnosis, a medication list or an insurance payer. Treat the AI agent as a business associate. Get a signed BAA from the voice provider, the LLM provider (Anthropic's Claude for Work and OpenAI's Business tier both offer them), the BSP and the CRM. Confirm the BAA names the AI features, not just the base phone or email service.

2. TCPA and 10DLC for outbound

Inbound is the low-risk starting point. The second the agent starts dialing or texting out, TCPA consent language, written capture, honored opt-outs and 10DLC Campaign Registry registration all apply. A healthcare prior-business-relationship exemption exists for treatment-related messages, but non-medical reactivation campaigns do not sit cleanly inside it. Work with counsel on the consent record; one record covering voice, SMS and WhatsApp is cleaner than three.

3. The explicit refuse-list

The agent must refuse and route for these every time:

  • Clinical advice of any kind: medication dosing, symptom triage, fall or injury assessment, wound description
  • Any statement that services are covered by Medicare Part A (they are not, for non-medical private duty) or that specific insurance will pay without a benefit check
  • Pricing negotiation beyond the agency's published bands
  • Promise of a specific caregiver by name
  • Any guarantee of outcome, safety or non-falls
  • Hospital-to-home transfers inside 24 hours without a care manager on the line
  • Hospice-level or skilled-nursing scope ("my dad needs a wound dressed")

4. Voice disclosure

Several states and the FCC's 2024-2026 updates require disclosing that the caller is speaking with an AI voice. The disclosure can be as simple as "You are speaking with Zenia, our AI assistant. A care manager can jump on the line any time, just say so." Put it in the opening turn, not buried in a long preamble.

2-week rollout plan

  1. Day 1-3. Discovery and data. Pull the last 180 days of calls, SMS and web form leads from WellSky or AxisCare. Map the top 8 inquiry types. Capture current intake coordinator's actual script (not the one in the handbook).
  2. Day 4-7. WhatsApp and web chat first. Launch the agent on WhatsApp and the site chat widget. Agent writes new leads to the CRM with source and transcript. No voice yet. Target: 95% of site visitors get a reply inside 60 seconds.
  3. Day 8-10. Voice. Port or forward the main number to the voice agent with a fallback to the coordinator's mobile for anything the agent flags as emergent. Opening disclosure in the first turn. Keep the answering service in the loop as a backup for the first two weeks, then retire it.
  4. Day 11-13. Reactivation and recruiting. Launch the 30/60/90 dormant family workflow against the last 12 months of inquiries. Launch the caregiver applicant intake flow. Both write to the CRM with structured records.
  5. Day 14. Review. Live-answer rate, first-reply time, inquiries captured, consults booked, started clients. Kill what did not work. Expand what did.

Day 30 and day 90 reviews follow the same template. By day 90 the only honest question is whether the agency can keep up with caregiver supply, because intake is no longer the bottleneck.

Who should not do this yet

Three cases where we tell agencies to wait:

  • Under 15 active clients. The volume does not justify the build. Use a tight voicemail script and a $5/call answering service.
  • EVV mandated and the current system has no open API (older HHAeXchange MLTSS deployments). The writeback cost kills the ROI.
  • No signed BAA available from the current CRM vendor. Do not work around it. Fix that first.
