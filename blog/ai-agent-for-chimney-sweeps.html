AI Agent for Chimney Sweeps: Fill the Fall Calendar Without Missing Calls

Chimney sweep demand collapses into about 20 weeks a year. From late August through the first hard freeze the phone will not stop ringing, and the rest of the year the schedule looks half empty. An AI agent on phone, WhatsApp, and web chat is what lets a 2-to-5-truck operation pick up every one of those fall calls, trigger the annual recall that fills January, and stop losing dormant customers to the sweep across town.

Why the chimney sweep calendar is broken

Vendor data and agency estimates line up on the same shape: bookings run hot from September through January and go quiet from April to August. One field-service vendor describes the fall rush as starting in late August and clearing out the calendar by the first cold snap. Smart Service recommends locking in seasonal hires by August at the latest because a new tech needs six to eight weeks of ride-alongs before they can run a Level 1 inspection alone.

That seasonal shape creates four problems the owner feels every year:

  • Missed fall calls. One owner on the truck plus one person at the desk cannot answer 40 to 80 inbound calls per day in October. Calls that go to voicemail at 8:15 a.m. become bookings for the competitor by 9:00 a.m.
  • No annual recall. NFPA 211 recommends an annual inspection for any venting system that burns solid fuel. Most sweeps never contact last year's customer in month 11. The customer books whoever shows up first in a search result.
  • Dormant customer database. A sweep with 5 years of jobs is sitting on 2,000 to 4,000 households who have not been touched. Industry reactivation benchmarks run from 5 to 15 percent on a single campaign.
  • Off-season cliff. April through August the phone rings maybe 6 times a day. Dryer vent, animal removal, waterproofing, cap and damper repair all exist as revenue, but the business is not marketing them and the office does not pitch them.

An AI agent does not fix the seasonality itself. It fixes the four leaks around it: the missed calls at peak, the recall nobody sends, the dormant list nobody works, and the off-season services nobody pitches.

What "AI agent" means for a 2-to-5-truck sweep

In this article an AI agent is the front desk, nothing more. It is a voice agent on the main business line, a WhatsApp Business API number on the website and Google Business Profile, a web chat on the site, and a shared layer behind them that books into the field-service calendar, writes into the CRM, and hands off to a human when the question is out of scope. It is not a phone tree, a generic voicemail-to-text tool, or a social-media auto-reply.

Built right, one agent handles all seven front-desk jobs below. Built wrong, you end up with three disconnected tools, three different voices, and a dispatcher who still has to re-ask every customer for their address.

The 7 jobs the AI agent actually does

  1. First-ring pickup at peak. Target: answer under 3 seconds, every call, 24/7, in English and Spanish. No ring groups, no voicemail during business hours. The agent captures name, address, fireplace type (wood, gas, pellet, insert), last sweep date, reason for the call, and any smoke or draft complaint.
  2. Qualification and routing. Routine Level 1 sweep plus visual inspection goes straight onto the calendar at the closest open slot. A real-estate sale, post-fire, post-quake, or appliance swap gets flagged as Level 2 (camera scan required) and routed to the senior tech. Suspected creosote stage 3 or a visible crack in the clay flue tile gets a "we need to see this before we quote" booking at a 30-minute diagnostic rate.
  3. Calendar-aware booking with deposit. The agent reads the field-service calendar's real availability, respects drive-zone rules (a 90-minute drive to a $200 sweep is not acceptable in October), and takes a $39 to $75 non-refundable deposit at booking. A Smart Service case study calls deposits the single most underused booking discipline in the category; a Contractor Plus case credits pre-pay for a sweep booking 6 months of fall work by mid-summer.
  4. Confirmation and no-show prevention. Three automated touches: 72 hours before (confirm the appointment and ask the homeowner to clear 4 feet around the hearth), 24 hours before (restate address and tech name), and 1 hour before (tech en route with ETA and a map link). Over WhatsApp the 24-hour touch typically clears north of 90 percent open rate; the 1-hour touch cuts the "nobody home" ride by more than half.
  5. Annual recall at month 11. Every homeowner who had a service last year gets a WhatsApp or SMS at month 11 with a single tap-to-book link and a $25 early-bird credit if they book before July 31. This is the single highest-ROI automation in the stack. A sweep with 1,800 active customers who books 22 percent of them at $285 average ticket is pulling $112k in work scheduled before Labor Day, which is when the fall rush starts.
  6. Dormant reactivation at 18 to 36 months. A sweep that has been running 5+ years has 2,000 to 4,000 households who skipped at least one year. A quarterly reactivation campaign pulled at the 18-, 24-, and 36-month marks typically recovers 5 to 10 percent per run with a modest offer ($20 off, or $99 cap-and-damper check-up). This is the April-through-August bread and butter.
  7. Off-season upsell. The same agent asks dryer vent, animal removal, waterproofing, and cap-replacement questions year-round. Dryer vent cleaning has a 12 to 18-month recall cycle that overlaps the quiet summer months; a $189 service on the back of a $285 sweep is pure margin. The agent does not negotiate price. It books a diagnostic or a flat-rate add-on and nothing else.

Explicit refuse-list: what the agent should never do

A good AI agent for a chimney sweep is boring on purpose. It books, confirms, and hands off. It does not improvise on any of the following:

  • No firm quotes on anything beyond flat-rate services (standard sweep plus Level 1, dryer vent, cap replacement with a pre-measured cap). Masonry repair, relining, insert install, and fire-damage work all require an onsite estimate.
  • No safety advice. If a caller describes active smoke, carbon monoxide symptoms, or a chimney fire in progress, the agent says "hang up and call 911" and texts a 911 reminder. No diagnosis over the phone.
  • No code interpretation. NFPA 211 and local code questions get a "our tech will confirm onsite" response. The agent does not state a clearance, a flue size, or a liner requirement.
  • No insurance-claim negotiation. Post-fire or post-storm work routes to a human estimator who handles the adjuster.
  • No HOA or landlord authority questions. If the caller is not the homeowner or legal tenant, the agent requires written authorization before scheduling.
  • No pricing negotiation. Published flat rates are the floor. Discounts route to the owner.

Write these rules into the system prompt and keep them there. Every time the agent gets caught making up a quote or a code answer, you lose a customer in a way that is harder to measure than a missed call.

2-week rollout, WhatsApp first, voice last

The mistake owners make is turning on the voice agent first. The voice stack has the most brittle failure modes (a bad transcription on an address kills the booking) and the highest stakes (a mishandled fire call is a public complaint). Start with the channel where mistakes are quietly fixable.

Week 1: data, WhatsApp, and the recall list.

  • Day 1-2: export the last 3 years of customer records from the field-service CRM. Normalize phone numbers, flag opt-outs, tag by last-service date and fireplace type.
  • Day 3: provision the WhatsApp Business API number and submit the 3 templates you need (annual recall, 24h confirmation, dormant reactivation). Approval runs 24 to 72 hours.
  • Day 4: start 10DLC registration for SMS fallback. This runs in the background for the next 2 to 4 weeks.
  • Day 5-7: wire the WhatsApp agent to the calendar. Test 20 real booking flows. Catch every edge case (apartment, HOA, unlisted street) before going live.

Week 2: web chat, voice, and the live run.

  • Day 8-9: web chat widget live on the site and Google Business Profile. Same backend as WhatsApp, so no duplicated scripts.
  • Day 10-11: voice agent live on a forwarded number (not the main business line yet). Shadow the dispatcher for 2 days. Review every call transcript end of day.
  • Day 12: switch the main business line to the voice agent for after-hours only. Keep the dispatcher on daytime for one more week.
  • Day 13-14: fire the annual recall campaign to everyone with a last-service date between 10 and 13 months ago. Fire the dormant reactivation to everyone between 18 and 36 months.

Day 30 review criteria: pickup rate above 95 percent on business hours and above 85 percent after-hours, call-to-booking above 45 percent, deposit take-rate above 75 percent, and no customer complaint about the agent misidentifying itself. Any one of those fails, the owner pulls the voice agent back to after-hours only and the vendor fixes before re-enabling.
