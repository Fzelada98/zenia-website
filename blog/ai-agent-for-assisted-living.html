AI Agent for Assisted Living: The 2026 US Playbook

Short answer: the daughter calls at 9:47 p.m. from the ED waiting room after her mother's second fall this month. Industry data from Vye's 2026 Senior Living Growth Insights Report shows 53% of senior living web inquiries get no reply inside two hours. Aline's Q2 2026 Benchmark Snapshot pegs assisted living inquiry-to-tour at 34% and tour-to-move-in at 46%, with web-sourced inquiries converting to a scheduled tour at roughly 57%. An AI agent on voice and WhatsApp that answers in under 60 seconds, qualifies against ADL needs and payer, books the tour into Yardi Senior CRM, Eldermark, ALIS, Welcomehome or Enquire, and runs a 30-70 day nurture is how an 85%-occupancy AL community moves toward the 90%+ primary-market average NIC MAP reported in Q3 2026.

Why assisted living intake is the lever in 2026

Assisted living reached 89.1% occupancy across NIC MAP's 31 Primary Markets in Q3 2026, up from 88.3% in Q2, with the segment gaining faster than independent living for the first time in several quarters and narrowing the IL/AL gap to 2.6 points. Overall senior housing occupancy crossed 90% for the first time since 2014. The supply-demand window is open: construction starts remain low, demographic demand is accelerating, and in-place rate growth for assisted living ran 5.3% YoY in June 2026 per NIC. The bottleneck is not demand. It is intake.

Vye's 2026 Senior Living Growth Insights Report puts 53% of web inquiries with no reply inside two hours. Aline's Q2 2026 Benchmark Snapshot shows assisted living inquiry-to-tour at 34% all-source and tour-to-move-in at 46% (down from 49% in Q2 2025), with web-sourced inquiries converting to a scheduled tour at about 57% for AL specifically. A MatrixCare brief citing Aline's annual report pegs end-to-end inquiry-to-move-in at approximately 4.3% industry-wide. That is the gap. The community that answers the 9:47 p.m. call from the ED is the community that captures the move-in. The one that returns the voicemail Tuesday morning is the one that explains to the ownership group why occupancy is still stuck at 82%.

Assisted living is also different from independent living in one operationally important way: the decision window compresses. A 2026 operator report cited an industry sales cycle that ran about 120 days from 2020 to 2026 and has stretched to 145 days in the most recent six months, but assisted living specifically shows urgency. Older Aline/NIC data put 57% of AL move-ins inside 30 days of inquiry, and a 2024 review found half of prospects move in within 24 days of tour. Hospital discharge, caregiver burnout, a fall, or a new dementia diagnosis is the event. The adult child is making a care decision on a deadline measured in days, not months.

What the AI agent actually does (8 intake jobs)

This is not a general-purpose assistant. It is a narrow operator with eight well-defined jobs, each with explicit guardrails that the sales counselor and clinical team review before go-live.

1. First-ring pickup, 24/7. Every inquiry path points at one number and one WhatsApp thread: direct inbound, A Place for Mom and Caring.com referral forms, website chat, hospital discharge planner, SNF social worker, physician office, family member. The agent answers in under 60 seconds across voice and messaging, including the 6 p.m. Friday to 9 a.m. Monday window that currently routes to voicemail or a legacy answering service with no community context.

2. Structured 9-field qualification. Prospect name and relationship (resident, adult child, spouse, discharge planner), care level estimate (independent with light support, 1-2 ADLs, 3+ ADLs, memory care crossover), mobility (independent, walker, wheelchair, transfer assist), continence, cognition flags, payer path (private pay, long-term care insurance, VA Aid and Attendance, Medicaid waiver where accepted, bridge loan), move-in urgency (hospital discharge inside 72 hours, next 30 days, 60-120 days, researching), geography and distance from decision-maker, and the single must-have (pet, specific unit type, religious services, cultural food). The agent captures the structure, reads it back verbatim, and writes it into Yardi Senior CRM, Eldermark, ALIS/Medtelligent, Welcomehome or Enquire through the community's existing API path or Zapier workflow.

3. Tour booking against the sales counselor calendar. The agent reads the sales counselor's calendar, blocks travel time for an in-person tour, offers a virtual tour as a first step for out-of-state adult children, and books directly. For a discharge-timed inquiry, it offers a same-day visit when a counselor is available and a nurse assessment slot inside 48 hours.

4. Pre-tour family nurture. Aline's data shows tour show rate is where senior living sales lose the most prospects between stages. The agent sends two scripted WhatsApp or SMS messages before the tour: what to bring (medication list, long-term care policy, ID), who will be there (sales counselor, director of resident care or wellness nurse for AL), what questions to expect on ADLs and cognition, and what the standard pricing structure looks like so the family is not blindsided at the conference table. Show rates in operator reports move from the 60-70% range to the high 80s with this single discipline.

5. Structured 30/60/90-day nurture across the assisted living decision window. For the researching prospect who is not in a 72-hour discharge sprint, the agent runs a four-touch cadence calibrated to AL's shorter window: day 3 (follow-up after first tour), day 10 (addressing the specific hesitation the counselor logged: pricing, care level, location), day 25 (second-tour invitation with the specific unit that matches the stated must-have), day 55 (last-check before the family defaults to a competitor or home care). Each touch is driven by the counselor's CRM note, not a generic template.

6. Deposit and move-in coordination. Once the deposit is signed, the agent manages the pre-move-in checklist: physician assessment form routed to the primary care provider, long-term care policy claim initiation, pharmacy transfer coordination, move-in day logistics (what time the moving company arrives, who meets the family at the door, when the care plan meeting is scheduled). Every step is tracked in the CRM and the family gets one WhatsApp thread that stays the single source of truth.

7. 6-month and 12-month dormant lead reactivation. Assisted living prospects who do not move in on the first pass often come back. Six months later the primary caregiver is burnt out, or there has been a second fall, or home care is no longer covering the acuity. The agent runs a scripted reactivation on a 6-month and 12-month cadence against families who toured but did not deposit, with permission-based WhatsApp templates and an explicit opt-out.

8. Caregiver recruiting intake. AL staffing is the industry's binding constraint. The same agent, with a different intent model, handles first-touch intake for caregiver, med tech, LPN and activities applicants against the community's background check and e-verification workflow, and books interviews with the Director of Resident Services.

Case study: 76-unit standalone AL in Columbus, Ohio

Independent operator, 76 licensed AL units (no IL, no standalone MC), Eldermark as EMR/care platform paired with a separate marketing CRM the counselor imported leads into by hand. Baseline occupancy was 82% (a 14-unit gap against 96% stabilized target) and the sales counselor was running flat for six months. Legacy answering service covered evenings and weekends with no community context.

Baseline numbers, trailing 90 days: 54 qualified inquiries per month across all sources, business-hours live-answer at 73%, after-hours live-answer at 0%, median first reply 3 hours 54 minutes, inquiry-to-tour at 28%, tour show rate at 64%, tour-to-deposit at 33%, move-ins at 2.4 per month, sales counselor spending 31 hours a week on phone work and data entry.

After 90 days live on the AI agent (voice + WhatsApp + web chat + inbound SMS, writing back to Eldermark through the vendor's structured import plus a Zapier bridge to the CRM): business-hours live-answer 99%, after-hours 96%, median first reply 51 seconds, inquiries moved to 79/month as web-sourced leads stopped leaking at the first-reply gap and three referral partners (a local hospital case management office and two home health agencies) reopened dormant pipelines, inquiry-to-tour 49%, tour show rate 86% after the pre-tour nurture discipline, tour-to-deposit 42%, move-ins 5.7/month. Over the 90 days, 11 dormant 6-to-18-month-old leads reactivated and deposited. Sales counselor time on phones and entry fell to 9 hours per week; the recovered time went to in-person referral-source visits with hospital case managers, which is where the pipeline opens.

Four compliance rails every AL community has to build around

HIPAA with a BAA scope that includes the model. Assisted living sits awkwardly on the HIPAA line. If the community is not billing Medicare Part A or acting as a covered entity in its own right, it is often not a HIPAA-covered entity directly. But the moment the AI agent handles a care-level discussion with an adult child, a hospital discharge planner's referral, or a long-term care insurance coordination call, PHI is in scope. The safe default: every vendor signs a BAA (voice, SMS/WhatsApp carrier, LLM, CRM, analytics), and the LLM path is explicitly inside it (Anthropic enterprise, OpenAI enterprise/ZDR, Google Vertex healthcare contracts). No BAA on the model, no PHI through the agent.

TCPA and 10DLC. Outbound SMS and WhatsApp to prospects and family members runs under TCPA. There is no healthcare marketing exemption; the 10DLC campaign registration and approved WhatsApp templates are the rails. The safest posture is inbound-first: the prospect opts in by texting the community, and outbound nurture is permission-based and opt-out-compliant. Every outbound template logs consent and template version.

Fair Housing Act and the 55+ HOPA exemption. Assisted living communities that qualify for the Housing for Older Persons Act 55+ exemption still operate under broader Fair Housing rules on race, national origin, religion, disability, family status and sex. The agent's refuse-list bans steering by any protected class. Disability accommodation requests route to a human within the business day.

State licensure and Medicaid waiver variability. Assisted living is state-licensed and rules vary materially: Texas Type A/B/C distinctions, California RCFE licensure, New York ALR/ALP structure, Florida Standard/ECC/LMH/LNS tiers, Ohio RCF licensing with the state's Assisted Living Waiver, Washington boarding home rules. The agent's state-aware policy layer carries the right disclosures, the specific care levels the community is licensed for, and the Medicaid waiver status (accepted, waitlist, not accepted) for that location. Hard-coded, not in free-text prompts.

The refuse-list (what the agent will not do)

An AL intake agent that tries to be helpful on the wrong topic is one that will cost the community a survey deficiency or a Fair Housing complaint. The standard refuse-list: diagnose or grade dementia, Alzheimer's or any clinical condition; promise the community can meet a specific care level without an in-person nurse assessment; guarantee acceptance of a Medicaid waiver placement without eligibility verification; quote a firm all-in monthly rate (base rent + care points) without the assessment on file; promise a specific room, view or floor without the sales counselor confirming availability; discuss internal staffing ratios, state-survey findings, incident reports or legal matters; steer on race, national origin, religion, disability or family structure; coordinate hospital discharge timing or clinical handoff beyond booking the admit visit; advise on long-term care insurance benefit decisions; or handle any call with active safety risk, suicidal ideation or disclosed elder abuse, which escalates to a human within the same message.

The FCC voice disclosure is in the opening turn of every outbound call and the first inbound message: "You are speaking with an automated assistant for [Community Name]. A sales counselor is available any time."

The 2-week rollout

Week 1, WhatsApp and web first. Stand up the WhatsApp Business API number, register 10DLC, approve the first six templates (inquiry receipt, tour scheduled, pre-tour prep, post-tour follow-up, 25-day nurture, dormant reactivation), wire the Yardi/Eldermark/ALIS/Welcomehome/Enquire write-back path, load the state licensure and Medicaid waiver policy layer, dry-run with the sales counselor and the director of resident services in parallel.

Week 2, voice goes live, phone last. Main line and after-hours line route through the voice agent with a bare-bones IVR fallback to the on-call manager. Pre-tour and 30/60/90 nurture cadences start on the active pipeline. Day-30 review criteria are concrete: live-answer rate, first-reply median, inquiry-to-tour, tour show rate, tour-to-deposit, trailing-30-day move-ins.

Who should not do this yet

Three honest cases: under 40 qualified inquiries per month, where the payback math is weak and a well-run sales counselor with a CRM discipline is enough; a CRM with a closed API and no appetite to pay for integration work, where without write-back the agent is a glorified voicemail and the counselor loses trust fast; and no BAA path with the LLM vendor compliance will approve, because any inquiry that touches care level, medication or diagnosis is PHI from the first turn.

What to measure in the first 90 days

Six numbers, reviewed weekly: live-answer rate split by business and after-hours, median first-reply time, inquiry-to-tour rate, tour show rate, tour-to-deposit rate, and trailing-30-day move-ins. If four of six are not moving by day 45, the problem is the integration, the script, or the sales counselor's trust in the hand-off. The agent is not the hard part. The hard part is making the sales counselor want to work alongside it, which comes down to CRM write-back fidelity and a refuse-list the counselor helped write.
