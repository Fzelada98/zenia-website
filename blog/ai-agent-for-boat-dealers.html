AI Agent for Boat Dealers: The 2026 Playbook

Short answer: US boat dealers lose most of their leads to slow follow up. Boating Industry put the useful first-contact window at 30 to 60 minutes and said conversion drops sharply after two hours, and a 2025 Powersports Business study found marine dealers set appointments on only 19 percent of sales calls versus 92 percent in automotive. An AI agent that answers the first inquiry inside a minute, pulls the trade-in and finance intake, and writes back into Lightspeed EVO, DX1, IDS or Rollick closes most of that gap. Inside: 3-store Florida case study, stack table, 2026 US pricing bands and a 14-day rollout.

Why marine dealerships bleed leads: the three gaps

Walk into a marine showroom on a Tuesday morning and the tension is easy to spot. Floor plan interest is pushing six figures a month on carried inventory, a sales desk that can seat four is covering six jobs, and the phone keeps ringing while the one person who can quote a Yamaha repower is on a boat test with a buyer. The missed-call list at 5pm is the shortlist of deals you did not close this week.

Three gaps show up in almost every US marine dealership I audit.

  • Speed to first reply. Boating Industry's long-standing guidance to marine dealers puts the useful first-contact window at 30 to 60 minutes, with conversion dropping sharply after two hours. The reality on the floor is a median first reply closer to 4 to 6 hours. For the Rollick, Boat Trader and manufacturer-site leads that arrive after 6pm or on a Sunday, the delay stretches to the next business day.
  • Appointment set rate on inbound calls. A 2025 Powersports Business study of pontoon sales calls (Sun Tracker-led) found dealers set appointments on only 19 percent of sales calls on average, against 77 percent in powersports and 92 percent in automotive. That is 4 out of 5 inbound sales calls that did not end with a calendar event.
  • Trade-in and finance intake quality. A boat deal needs HIN, year, make, model, hours, loan payoff, trailer and sometimes engine make, model and hours separately. A hurried salesperson writes 3 of those on a sticky note. The F and I desk then spends a full day chasing the owner for the rest before a credit app can go to Yamaha Motor Finance, M and T, Priority One or LightStream.

An AI agent does not replace the sales desk. It stops those three gaps from eating the pipeline between ad spend and the quote.

What an AI agent for boat dealers actually does

Keep the definition narrow. An AI agent for a marine dealership is a WhatsApp, SMS, web-chat and voice front end that owns the first hour of every new inquiry and the follow-up cadence on open opportunities. It does seven jobs.

  1. First-ring pickup, 24/7. Answers inbound SMS, WhatsApp, Facebook Messenger, Instagram DM, Rollick and Boat Trader lead posts, and website chat inside 30 to 60 seconds with a boat-specific greeting that references the exact unit the shopper looked at.
  2. Qualification in the shopper's native channel. New or pre-owned, use case (fishing, cruising, wakeboarding, pontoon family), budget band, financing or cash, trade-in yes or no, home state for tax and titling, timeline. Six questions, not a form.
  3. Trade-in intake. Collects year, make, model, length, engine make and model, engine hours, trailer yes or no, loan payoff estimate and three photos (bow, console, hull sticker with HIN). Routes it to a human for a NADA or J.D. Power Boat Guide lookup.
  4. Finance pre-qual handoff. Captures the soft credit range, down payment, desired term and routes it to the F and I desk with the lender the dealer prefers for that band (Yamaha Motor Finance for OEM-backed, Priority One or Medallion for mid-tier, LightStream or local credit union for prime).
  5. Calendar write. Books sea trials, in-showroom walk-throughs and service drop-offs directly on the sales rep's Google or Outlook calendar, honoring the launch ramp schedule.
  6. Reminder and no-show rescue. 48h, 24h, 2h and 30-min cadence before a sea trial (weather, slip number, what to bring), then a 15-minute post-no-show rescue with a one-click reschedule.
  7. Dormant reactivation. At 30, 60 and 90 days, touches shoppers who did not buy with a reason-to-return (new inventory match, model-year price change, service bay opening for repower). An MRAA piece put it plainly: lead nurture is no longer optional.

What it does not do: negotiate price, make warranty rulings, approve credit, recommend a specific repower against a competitor's engine, or talk to a Coast Guard inquiry. Those are human calls, and the agent hands them off with full context already captured.

Case study: 3-store Florida pontoon and center-console dealer, 90 days

A Tampa Bay group with three showrooms (one on the Gulf, one inland, one in Clearwater), 42 units moved per month pre-rollout, mixed line-card (pontoon, bay, offshore center console, PWC), 4 sales reps and 2 F and I managers. Rollick, Boat Trader, Google local, manufacturer-site referrals and walk-ins. The owner wanted appointment set rate up and after-hours leakage down before summer.

Before the rollout (trailing 90 days, pulled from Lightspeed EVO and the website analytics):

  • Median first reply on digital leads: 4h 11m during business hours, 11h 38m after hours and on Sunday
  • Appointment set rate on inbound sales calls: 22 percent (in line with the 19 percent Powersports Business study)
  • Trade-in packet completeness at first pass: 31 percent (meaning 69 percent of trades needed a second owner call to close the intake)
  • Credit application turnaround from lead to lender submit: 2.4 business days
  • Sea-trial no-show rate: 24 percent
  • Dormant 30-90 day leads reactivated per month: 3

After 90 days (AI agent across WhatsApp, SMS, web chat and Facebook/IG DM, writing into Lightspeed EVO, honoring Rollick lead posts, with Yamaha Motor Finance and Priority One pre-qual routing):

  • Median first reply: 38 seconds, 24/7 (the measured p95 was 1m 42s)
  • Appointment set rate on inbound: 53 percent (manual on complex trade-ins, agent-led on the rest)
  • Trade-in packet completeness at first pass: 84 percent (year, make, model, length, engine, hours, loan payoff range, 3 photos including HIN sticker)
  • Credit application turnaround: 0.6 business days
  • Sea-trial no-show rate: 11 percent (24h plus 2h plus 30m cadence with ramp info and weather)
  • Dormant reactivations per month: 18, with 6 closed at an average of $47,200 per unit

What that is worth. Units moved per month went from 42 to 54 over the second half of the pilot, an increment of 12 units at an average gross of roughly $8,400 per unit (new and pre-owned blended, before F and I reserve). That is about $100,800 a month in incremental front-end gross, with F and I reserve adding another $14,000 to $18,000 on top at a 60 percent penetration. The all-in build cost was $9,400 setup plus $1,895 per month (WhatsApp Business API provider, Twilio voice and SMS, model inference, Lightspeed EVO sync and support). Full payback inside the first month, and the F and I team got back roughly 11 hours a week of chase-the-owner time.

A marine dealership's technology stack is more fragmented than a car dealer's. There is no single DMS that dominates, and the website, CRM and DMS often come from three different vendors. The AI agent has to sit in the middle without demanding anyone replace what they already own.

What matters for the AI agent build is not which DMS wins. It is that the DMS exposes an inventory feed and a deal write-back endpoint. If both exist, the agent can greet a shopper by the exact unit and later create a deal record a salesperson picks up without re-keying anything.

The six inquiry types an agent has to handle well

A boat dealer's inbound mix is more diverse than a car dealer's. Script these six and the agent covers 90 percent of the first-contact traffic.

  1. New boat shopper, specific unit. Shopper clicked a Rollick or Boat Trader listing. Greet by model, confirm new vs pre-owned, ask use case, budget band, trade-in yes or no, timeline. Book a showroom or sea trial.
  2. New boat shopper, open brief. Shopper lands on the dealer's website and asks about pontoons under $65,000. Narrow with 3 questions (water type, passenger count, tow or no-tow), return 2 to 3 matches from the inventory feed, offer a sea trial on the top pick.
  3. Pre-owned trade or consignment. Shopper wants to sell a 2017 Sea-Ray SDX 270. Run the trade-in intake script, flag for the used-boat manager, do not quote a number.
  4. Service and warranty. Shopper needs a 100-hour service on a Yamaha F300. Pull last service record from the DMS if the HIN matches, offer three slots at the right store, confirm drop-off and pickup.
  5. Parts and accessories. Shopper wants a bimini top or a specific SeaStar cable. Check parts inventory, quote, offer local pickup or ship-to.
  6. Financing question only. Shopper wants to know what rate they can get on a $120,000 unit at 20 percent down for a 15-year term. Hand off to F and I with soft credit range captured; do not quote rates directly.

The scripts are short on purpose. A marine shopper on their phone during lunch break will not finish a 12-question form, and the agent should look like a friendly dealer, not a canned script. Every path ends either on a human handoff or a booked calendar event.

What boat dealers specifically need in the build (that generic AI tools miss)

Most AI tools sold to dealers were built for cars and retrofitted for marine. The marine-specific requirements are real.

  • HIN capture, not VIN. Hull Identification Number is 12 characters, assigned by the US Coast Guard, and shows up as a sticker on the starboard transom. The agent has to ask for a photo, not type the number, and has to recognize that Boston Whaler, Sea-Ray and Yamaha number their units differently.
  • Engine hours are a first-class field. On a pre-owned boat, engine hours drive valuation more than mileage does on a car. Capture engine make, model and hours separately from the hull, and capture it twice on twin-engine setups.
  • Trailer yes or no and trailer brand. A 2019 24-foot center console with an aluminum Magic Tilt trailer is a different unit from the same boat on a 2011 bunk trailer. The agent asks, the DMS stores it, the buyer sees it in the listing.
  • Sea trial slots are not showroom slots. A sea trial depends on weather, tide, ramp availability and the demo boat being clean and fueled. The agent books against a sea-trial calendar that is distinct from the sales rep's showroom calendar, and sends the ramp address, slip number and weather forecast in the 24h reminder.
  • State-specific titling and tax. Florida sales tax is capped at $18,000 on boats. Texas uses a 6.25 percent cap on the first $18,750 (as of the most recent rules; verify before quoting). California does not cap. The agent captures home state and routes the right disclosure, and does not quote exact tax.
  • Marine survey requirement. Lenders typically require a marine survey for any boat over $25,000 or 25 feet. The agent should know this, and should proactively route the surveyor booking after the deposit, not after the contract.
  • Winter vs summer cadence. In the Great Lakes and Northeast, service drives Q4 and Q1 traffic; sales drives Q2 and Q3. In Florida, Texas and California, sales runs year-round. The reactivation cadence has to shift by region.

US pricing bands for a 2026 AI agent build

Numbers below are what a US marine dealer should expect to see from a competent build partner, not from a generic SaaS seat. They include the WhatsApp Business API provider pass-through, Twilio voice and SMS, model inference and a reasonable amount of integration work.

  • Single-store dealer, 1 to 15 units a month: $4,900 to $7,500 setup, $699 to $1,195 per month
  • Multi-store dealer, 2 to 5 locations, 15 to 60 units a month: $8,500 to $14,500 setup, $1,495 to $2,495 per month
  • Large group, 6 or more locations or 60+ units a month: $18,000 to $35,000 setup, $2,995 to $5,500 per month
  • Pure pre-owned brokerage, no new-boat franchise: $4,500 to $6,500 setup, $595 to $995 per month

A second BDC (business development center) agent runs $4,500 to $5,500 per month fully loaded in most US markets (salary, taxes, benefits, workstation). The math works in favor of the AI agent build on month three for most multi-store dealers. For a one-store, the comparison is less obvious and the honest answer is: build it for speed to first reply and after-hours coverage, not to replace the BDC seat.

A 14-day rollout, in the order it actually ships

Treat the rollout the way you would treat a new outboard install. Measured, in order, and tested before anyone relies on it.

Days 1 to 3: audit and connect

  • Pull the last 90 days of leads from the DMS. Sort by source. Measure baseline first-reply, set rate, no-show rate and dormant count.
  • Connect the WhatsApp Business number through a Meta-approved BSP. Verify the dealership name as a display name.
  • Wire the Rollick, Boat Trader, YachtWorld and manufacturer-site webhooks into the agent inbox. Confirm dedupe against the DMS.
  • Read-only connection to the DMS inventory feed. Confirm unit URLs, prices, photos and spec fields come through intact.

Days 4 to 7: script and sea-trial calendar

  • Write the six inquiry scripts in the dealer's voice. Review line by line with the sales manager, not with the IT vendor.
  • Set up the sea-trial calendar as a separate resource with slot rules (ramp open hours, weather hold, fuel check).
  • Wire the trade-in intake with the photo requirements. Test with 3 real pre-owned leads from the last week.
  • Set the finance pre-qual routing: which lender takes which credit band, what fields to capture, who at the F and I desk is on the handoff.

Days 8 to 11: shadow mode

  • Run the agent in shadow mode for 72 hours. It drafts replies, a human sends them. Track the catch rate (how often the human overrode the draft) and the miss rate (how often the human said something the agent did not have a path for).
  • Move to assisted mode: the agent sends automatically during business hours, a human reviews after-hours replies the next morning.
  • Train the sales desk on the handoff. 5 minutes per rep, not a 2-hour PowerPoint.

Days 12 to 14: live and measure

  • Full 24/7 coverage. Keep a human on-call for Sunday afternoons during peak season.
  • Review the first 48 hours live every day for the first week. Hunt for the three or four edge cases that only show up in production.
  • Set the day-30, day-60 and day-90 review criteria now (not later). The four numbers that matter: median first reply, appointment set rate, trade-in packet completeness, lead-to-lender-submit time.

Dealers who try to roll out all six inquiry types on day one usually regret it. The service and parts paths are the two that need the most tuning, and they are safe to defer to week three without hurting the sales-side payback.

What this does not fix

An AI agent is not the answer to every marine dealership problem. Being honest about that matters.

  • It does not change what a shopper will pay for a boat. Price objections still land on a human.
  • It does not fix a shortage of inventory. If a dealer's Yamaha allocation is short, the agent cannot conjure units.
  • It does not replace the F and I manager. Credit app packaging, back-end product stacking and lender negotiation are still a human job.
  • It does not replace the service writer. A diagnostic conversation on a Yamaha F300 fault code needs a real tech.
  • It does not replace sea trials. Boats are bought on the water, not on a quote.

The agent fixes the one thing that matters most upstream of all five of those: making sure every shopper gets a prompt, specific, boat-aware reply inside a minute, so none of the humans downstream are starting from scratch.
