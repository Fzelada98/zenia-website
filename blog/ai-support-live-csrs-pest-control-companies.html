AI to Support Live CSRs at Pest Control Companies

Most pest control AI pitches try to replace the person on the phone. That is the wrong fight for a seasonal, trust-driven service. The better move is a copilot that sits next to a human CSR, pulls the right answer in under a second, and writes the after-call notes while they take the next call. Here is what that looks like in 2026, with the stack, the metrics, and a two-week rollout.

The CSR seat is the one you cannot afford to lose

Pest control runs on recurring revenue. Quarterly plans, mosquito seasonal, termite renewals: roughly 85% of a healthy operator's revenue in the US comes from existing customers, and almost all of that revenue touches the CSR seat at least four times a year. Scheduling the next treatment, handling a reschedule, taking a callback request, upselling into rodent exclusion or wildlife, cancelling before the next charge: every one of those is a human conversation on a phone or a WhatsApp thread.

Replicant, one of the few vendors with named pest control deployments, pitched its product in 2023 after a Fortune-ranked operator could not scale its CSR team fast enough through the spring call spike. The pain was not that calls were going unanswered. It was that call volume doubles from February to May, and hiring 15 seasonal CSRs that behave like veterans is impossible. Rentokil, Terminix, and the mid-market independents all live with the same curve.

The reflex response of the vendor market has been to sell an AI receptionist that answers the call end to end. For 60-70% of true after-hours overflow and simple reschedules, that is the right tool. For the other 30-40% (an angry commercial account, a technician no-show complaint, a $4,800 termite proposal, a customer who wants to cancel), the right tool is still a CSR. The question is how to make that CSR 30-40% more productive without burning them out.

What "AI to support live CSRs" actually means

Two product categories are getting flattened into one word, and operators keep buying the wrong one.

AI receptionist (voice agent): picks up the line, qualifies, books, and transfers. Replaces headcount. Good for overflow, after-hours, lower-tier calls. Sold by vendors like Solea AI, CallSphere, callin.io, and the "AI phone" layer bundled into FieldRoutes and Jobber.

AI copilot (agent assist): listens to a live human CSR, surfaces the next-best answer, drafts the follow-up SMS, and writes the CRM wrap-up. Does not touch the customer. Augments headcount. Sold by Cresta, Observe.AI, Dialpad Ai, and a thin layer of pest-specific tools riding on top of PestPac, FieldRoutes, or GorillaDesk APIs.

Both can exist in the same operation. The copilot is the piece that pays back fastest for an operator who already has a CSR team of 4-20 people and wants to keep them.

The four CSR tasks a copilot handles well in pest control

1. Real-time knowledge retrieval

A CSR gets a call from a Tucson customer asking whether the quarterly plan covers scorpions. Without a copilot, that is 30-90 seconds in PestPac, a slack message to the office manager, or a guess. With a copilot listening to the audio, the plan definition and service-area notes surface on screen before the customer finishes the sentence. The CSR reads it out, confirms coverage, and books the add-on in the same call.

What the copilot actually needs to retrieve, in priority order:

  • Plan definitions and inclusions per state and service area (termite rules vary by Texas county, scorpion coverage varies by Arizona ZIP)
  • Pricing bands and current promotions (quarterly, bi-monthly, one-time, commercial)
  • Product and chemical answers for pet-safe, pregnancy-safe, food-service questions
  • Technician availability for the next 10 days on this account's route
  • The customer's own history: last three services, open callbacks, outstanding invoices, prior cancellation attempts

Agent-assist platforms (Cresta, Dialpad, custom builds) hit 400-900 ms retrieval latency on this kind of structured content with a small RAG index. Keep the index to under 2,000 vetted answers; a bigger one gets slower and starts hallucinating pricing.

2. Live transcription and sentiment flags

The copilot transcribes both sides of the call and watches for three signals that trigger a next action: cancellation intent, callback or re-service escalation, and a complaint about a specific technician. Each signal fires a different workflow. Cancellation intent routes to a save script (quarterly to bi-monthly downgrade, one month free, swap for pest-only without termite). Re-service escalation pre-books a tech within 48 hours and flags dispatch. A technician complaint opens a QA ticket for the ops manager before the call ends.

Two things to watch here. First, keep the sentiment model simple: intent detection is reliable, emotion scoring is not. Second, do not auto-send anything to the customer during the live call; the CSR owns that moment.

3. After-call work (ACW) wrap-up

This is where the copilot earns its keep. In a typical pest control CSR workday, after-call work is 30-45 seconds per call: write a one-line note, update the service address if changed, set a callback, log the chemical a tech used last visit, confirm an upsell. Across 60-90 calls a day, that is 30-67 minutes of pure data entry.

A copilot drafts the wrap-up from the live transcript, pre-fills the CRM fields (PestPac, FieldRoutes, GorillaDesk, Briostack, or ServicePro), and asks the CSR for a one-click confirm. Average ACW drops from 35-45 seconds to 8-12 seconds. On a 20-person CSR team at 70 calls a day, that is 7-11 agent-hours per day recovered: roughly 1.0 to 1.5 FTE of capacity without hiring.

4. Script coaching and QA on 100% of calls

Pest control call QA is still mostly manual, with a supervisor listening to 2-5% of calls per CSR per month. A copilot can score 100% of calls against a simple rubric: did the CSR confirm the service address, offer the annual plan when appropriate, mention the guarantee, upsell rodent or mosquito when it fit, close with the next-visit date. QA scores feed a weekly dashboard that is useful instead of punitive.

The upside is not the dashboard. It is that CSRs stop missing the 10% of calls where a $240/year quarterly plan was obviously on the table and nobody asked.

The stack: what plugs into what

A pest control CSR copilot has four pieces, and they are all off-the-shelf in 2026.

  • Telephony and recording: RingCentral, Dialpad, Aircall, or the phone layer already in FieldRoutes or PestPac. The copilot needs the live audio stream (SIPREC or a WebRTC side-car) and both-channel recording.
  • Transcription: Deepgram, AssemblyAI, or Dialpad's built-in ASR. Target under 500 ms end-of-utterance latency and sub-10% WER on US accents in a noisy call-center room. Spanish-English code switching matters in Texas, Arizona, Florida, and California; make sure the ASR handles it without a language switch.
  • Knowledge and retrieval: a RAG index built from plan docs, service-area rules, technician schedules via API, and the last 90 days of customer history. Hosted on Pinecone, Weaviate, or a Postgres pgvector table; the choice matters less than the content hygiene.
  • CRM write-back: PestPac SOAP/REST, FieldRoutes API, GorillaDesk API, or ServicePro. The copilot writes a draft note, a disposition, a follow-up task, and (if present) a Twilio or WhatsApp Business API follow-up message queued for CSR approval.

The missing piece most operators ignore is the audit trail. Every suggestion the copilot gave, every one the CSR accepted or rejected, and every CRM field it wrote should be logged. That log is the only honest way to tune the copilot after week one.

A two-week rollout for a 10-seat CSR team

No AI copilot survives a six-month implementation plan in pest control. Call volume is seasonal, and if you miss the window, you have waited another year. This is the fastest honest rollout for a 10-seat team on PestPac or FieldRoutes.

  1. Days 1-3: wire telephony to the copilot (SIPREC or WebRTC side-car), confirm both-channel audio and under-500 ms transcription latency. Pull a seed export of the last 90 days of CRM data for the retrieval index.
  2. Days 4-6: build the knowledge base. 150-250 vetted answers covering plans, pricing, service areas, pet and food-service safety, cancellation scripts, and the top 20 technician-specific scenarios. Trim aggressively; small and clean beats big and noisy.
  3. Days 7-8: integrate CRM write-back. Draft a wrap-up template per disposition code and run it dry against last week's calls to validate.
  4. Days 9-10: shadow mode. Copilot runs on live calls but only the two senior CSRs see suggestions. Collect accept-reject logs; retune retrieval.
  5. Day 11: full rollout to the 10-seat team with a 45-minute training session. Keep the ops manager in a listen-only room for the first morning.
  6. Days 12-14: daily retune. Pull the top 20 rejected suggestions each night and fix the knowledge base. By end of week two, the acceptance rate on retrieval should be above 70%.

Budget for a 10-seat team: $4,000-$7,000 setup, $35-$75 per seat per month on the SaaS layer, plus the telephony cost you already pay. Payback inside the first season when AHT drops 10%+ and ACW drops 50%+.

Common mistakes operators keep making

  • Buying a receptionist and calling it a copilot. A voice agent that answers the call is not an agent-assist tool. If your CSRs never see the AI, it is not a copilot.
  • Loading the knowledge base with 5,000 PDFs. Retrieval accuracy drops hard above 2,000 entries. Curate.
  • Letting the copilot auto-send WhatsApp or SMS during the live call. The CSR owns the conversation. The copilot drafts; the CSR clicks send.
  • Skipping the QA layer. If you do not score 100% of calls, you cannot tune the copilot. You will also not catch the CSRs who are quietly stopping the upsell.
  • Measuring only AHT. AHT is a lagging number. Watch acceptance rate on retrieval, FCR, plan-attach rate, and 90-day CSR retention. AHT follows.
  • Buying a tool without CRM write-back. If the copilot does not write the wrap-up into PestPac or FieldRoutes, you have not saved the 30 seconds. You have just moved them.

What this looks like from a CSR's seat

A Tuesday morning call comes in. The copilot pulls up the customer's last three services, the open termite proposal from six weeks ago, and the fact that the account went from quarterly to bi-monthly last July. On screen, three suggested openings sit above the transcript. The CSR picks one, confirms the next service date, and sees a soft prompt: "mention the mosquito seasonal, this ZIP has a 41% attach rate." The CSR offers, the customer accepts, and the copilot drops a 2-line wrap-up in PestPac with the correct disposition. Call ends 2:48 after it started. ACW is 6 seconds. The CSR takes the next call.
