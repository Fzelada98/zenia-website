AI Receptionist for Chiropractors: The 2026 US Practice Playbook

A chiropractic practice has a scheduling problem every other industry would call a crisis. The doctor has hands on a patient between 9:00 and 11:30, again from 1:30 to 5:00, and the phone rings during adjustments. If you have one CA at the front desk she is checking in patients, verifying insurance, running payments, and reminding people about their next visit. The phone is the fifth thing on her list, and it loses. This guide covers what an AI receptionist actually does for a US chiropractic office, what it does not try to do, how it hooks into ChiroTouch, Genesis, ChiroSpring, and the real numbers from a 2-DC practice in Charlotte.

Why the chiropractic phone is different from a dentist's phone

Chiropractic has three structural quirks that make the front desk harder than it looks.

First, the new-patient call is unusually long. A new patient on a dental call wants a cleaning date. A new patient on a chiropractic call wants to tell you about their lower back, ask whether you take their PPO, confirm you accept Medicare, understand what a Report of Findings is, and figure out if the first visit is $65, $195, or a complimentary consult with X-rays included. A well-run new-patient call is 7 to 11 minutes. A rushed one converts at half the rate.

Second, the schedule is high-frequency. A regular care patient comes in 2 to 3 times a week for the first month, then tapers. A single CA is coordinating 60 to 120 touch points a week on repeat patients while trying to intake the 8 to 20 new patients that pay for the month. The phone is competing with check-in scans, SOAP note signatures, and the inserts that Medicare asks for on 97012 and 97140.

Third, chiropractic does not separate front desk from phones the way medical specialties do. A dermatology clinic has a scheduler in a back room and a receptionist in the lobby. A chiropractic office has one person doing both, and that person is standing. She cannot wear a headset for 6 hours and she cannot leave the patient at the counter to pick up ring 2.

Those three facts are why the industry average for missed new-patient calls in solo and 2-DC practices runs in the 30 to 40 percent range (figures circulated by scheduling vendors; your own phone log is the only honest source). At $75 to $200 in lost first-visit revenue per missed call, with downstream lifetime value in the $2,400 to $4,800 range, missing one new-patient call a day quietly compounds into the difference between a growing practice and a flat one.

What an AI receptionist actually does in a chiropractic office

The role is narrower than the marketing copy suggests. An AI receptionist is the phone, WhatsApp, Instagram DM, and website chat layer of the front desk. It is not an EHR, it is not a SOAP note assistant, and it does not touch the clinical side of your day. Scope it that way and it works. Expand it and it breaks.

1. Answers every inbound call on the first ring

The receptionist sits behind your existing office number via a forward or a direct port. It picks up in English or Spanish, identifies itself as the office's AI assistant, and runs a short intent script. For roughly 70 to 85 percent of calls the full conversation never needs a human: patients want appointment times, pricing for self-pay packages, directions, whether the office is open on Saturday, and whether their PPO is in-network. The agent reads those off your live price table and schedule.

2. Qualifies new patients the way a trained CA would

The script runs seven fields in a conversational order, not a checklist: name, date of birth, chief complaint (lower back, neck, headaches, auto accident, work injury, extremity), onset (acute versus chronic), referral source, insurance carrier and member ID, and whether this is cash-pay or insurance-first. A chiropractic office that accepts PI cases adds crash date and police report Y/N. The agent then matches the patient to a new-patient slot that fits your doctor's schedule for intakes (most practices block two to four 45-minute new-patient slots a day; the agent respects that).

3. Books directly into your practice management system

The agent writes into ChiroTouch, Genesis Chiropractic, ChiroSpring, ChiroFusion, zHealth, Jane, or whichever platform you run. Double-booking is impossible because it reads the live calendar. New patients get a confirmation text with the forms link (OATS/NDI/ODI intake, HIPAA notice, financial policy) and a 24-hour reminder. Return patients get a one-tap confirm or reschedule.

4. Handles the 2-3 visits-a-week cadence that defines chiropractic

This is the piece that generic AI receptionists get wrong. A chiropractic patient on a care plan has a frequency, not an appointment. The agent knows the plan ("3x/wk for 4 weeks, then 2x/wk for 4 weeks, then 1x/wk for 4 weeks") and offers the next three to five visits as a block, not one at a time. Rebooking at check-out moves from a 90-second conversation to a tap on the patient's phone after they leave.

5. Verifies insurance before the visit, not at the counter

For PPOs with a real-time eligibility feed (most of the big ones: BCBS, Aetna, United, Cigna, Humana), the agent runs eligibility on booking and tells the patient their estimated copay and deductible status before they walk in. For plans without a feed, it drops a verification task in the CA queue with the right phone number pre-filled. The dollar impact is small on any one visit; across a month it is the difference between a clean AR and a stack of patient balances the CA has to chase.

6. Recalls dormant patients on a schedule, not a whim

The engine tags any active-care patient who has missed a scheduled visit by 14, 30, 60, and 90 days. Different messages go to each cohort. A 14-day lapse gets a soft "hope everything is still feeling good, let's close out your plan." A 90-day lapse gets a "we have a new patient opening this week, want to come back in for a reassessment." On a typical 2-DC practice that is 180 to 240 names at any moment, and the CA has not had time to work the list in 18 months.

7. Hands off cleanly when a human is required

The agent does not pretend to be the doctor and does not pretend to be a nurse. Clinical questions ("can I come in if my sciatica is shooting down my leg into the toes?"), billing disputes, and anything involving a minor without a parent on the line route to the DC or office manager with a 30-second transcript already attached. During adjustments those pages go to voicemail; the DC gets a stacked text at the end of the hour.

Things the agent does not do, and should not

The list of refusals is as important as the list of jobs. The agent does not give clinical advice, does not negotiate price, does not interpret X-rays, does not promise outcomes, and does not discuss anyone's PHI with a caller it has not verified. It does not accept a new patient with a chief complaint that is outside scope (fracture, suspected stroke, cauda equina symptoms, acute post-surgical pain) and routes those calls to the DC with a flag. It does not accept telehealth-only requests in states where chiropractic telehealth is restricted. It does not take disputes about a bill; those go to the office manager.

HIPAA and the one thing most vendors get wrong

Any vendor handling patient-identifying content for your practice must sign a Business Associate Agreement. The call recording, the transcript, the WhatsApp thread, and the eligibility check all contain PHI by default. Three practical rules:

  • Signed BAA before go-live. From Twilio or whichever carrier sits under your voice, from the LLM provider (Anthropic has a BAA on enterprise plans; so does OpenAI), from the eligibility clearinghouse, and from the forms vendor. If any one refuses to sign, swap them out.
  • Retention policy on recordings. Default to 30 or 60 days then purge, unless your state (TX, FL, CA for PI cases) requires longer. Store transcripts separately from audio.
  • Role-based access on the admin console. The CA sees everything; the massage therapist sees nothing. The outside bookkeeper sees nothing clinical. Audit log on by default.

The same discipline that keeps your chiropractor CRM compliant applies at the receptionist layer: BAA, retention, access, log.

Two-week rollout, chiropractic edition

  1. Day 1 to 2: data audit. Export 60 days of ChiroTouch or Genesis call logs, no-shows, and new-patient intake. Pick the two or three real bottlenecks. For most offices they are new-patient call abandonment, after-hours, and the dormant list.
  2. Day 3: number provisioning. Keep your office number. A Twilio or Vonage number sits behind it for the AI receptionist. Call forwarding is set to "busy and no-answer" first, "all" at day 8.
  3. Day 4 to 5: script and refuse list. The DC approves the scope line by line: what the agent books, what it quotes, what it hands off. Red-line everything.
  4. Day 6: PMS writeback test. 10 fake appointments pushed into ChiroTouch or Genesis. Fix the API glue before any real patient touches it.
  5. Day 7 to 8: WhatsApp and SMS templates. Meta approves two to four templates for confirmations, reminders, and intake forms. 10DLC registration starts in parallel (allow 3 to 10 business days).
  6. Day 9 to 10: dry-run testing. 30 to 50 test calls through the agent, including accents, bad connections, two Spanish calls, one combative caller. The DC listens to the transcripts.
  7. Day 11: go live on overflow. Agent answers only when the CA is on another line or past 5 rings. Everyone keeps their day job.
  8. Day 15 onward: promote to primary. Agent answers everything. The CA now works the dormant list, intakes forms, and runs eligibility on the next day's schedule. The DC reads a Monday-morning dashboard: calls, new patients, no-shows, reactivations, ROF conversion.
