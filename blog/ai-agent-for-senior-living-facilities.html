AI Agent for Senior Living Facilities: The 2026 US Playbook

Short answer: inquiry-to-move-in across US senior living averages about 4.3% (Aline 2026), and 53% of web inquiries get no response inside two hours (Vye 2026 Growth Insights Report). The adult daughter who fills out your tour form at 9:47pm on a Tuesday is comparing you against two other communities before Wednesday breakfast. An AI agent that answers in under 60 seconds, qualifies care level and payer, books a tour into Enquire, Yardi RentCafe Senior CRM, Eldermark or ALIS, and runs a disciplined 4-touch nurture across a 70 to 120 day decision window is the single highest-leverage change an operator can make in 2026. Inside: 8 jobs the agent does, a 140-unit Raleigh IL/AL case with a move from 68% to 86% occupancy, the stack, the refuse-list that keeps an operator clear of HIPAA and fair housing trouble, 2026 pricing bands and a 2-week rollout.

Why senior living move-ins leak at the top of the funnel

The macro picture is what every operator already knows. Aline's 2025 operator survey identified occupancy as the leading concern; the 2026 Q2 Sales & Marketing Benchmark Snapshot put the industry inquiry-to-move-in rate at roughly 4.3%, with inquiry-to-tour between 27% (life plan) and 36% (memory care), and tour-to-move-in between 29% and 52% depending on care type. A separate Siro analysis of 18-state operator data put tour-to-move-in flat at 23.7%. The variance tells you how wide the operator spread is.

What those numbers mean on a Tuesday night: the community that answers the first inquiry fast and keeps the family inside a multi-touch sequence for the full decision window wins the move-in. Everyone else pays a referral fee to A Place for Mom on the ones that convert and loses the rest.

Three facts that explain where the leak sits:

  • The adult daughter is the buyer, and she inquires after hours. Vendor call-pattern data across the category consistently shows the majority of first inquiries arrive outside 9am-5pm Monday-Friday. The adult child is between a full-time job and a parent crisis.
  • The decision window is long. Hyperleap, citing CCR Growth, puts the average inquiry-to-move-in at around 120 days for independent living and around 70 days for assisted living. The nurture job is a structured 70 to 120 day job, not a 48-hour follow-up.
  • Response time is the biggest control variable. 53% of web inquiries get no reply inside two hours (Vye 2026), and another 13% of mystery-shop calls to communities never received a response at all (Bild & Co. data, re-reported in 2023-2024 and still cited in 2026 operator trainings).

None of that gets fixed by hiring a second sales counselor. It gets fixed by putting an AI agent in front of voice, SMS, WhatsApp and web chat, with a disciplined handoff to the sales counselor at the right moment in the funnel.

What an AI agent actually does for a senior living community (8 jobs)

By "AI agent" we mean one assistant that lives across inbound phone, outbound dial-outs, SMS, WhatsApp, Instagram DM and the website chat widget, writes structured records back to the senior living CRM, and hands off to a human sales counselor at a defined point. Not a tree-based FAQ widget. Not a generic answering service that reads a card.

1. First-ring pickup, 24/7, with the right opening tone

The agent picks up every inbound call in under 3 seconds, greets by community name, and speaks at the pace an 82-year-old or an adult child in a hospital hallway can actually follow. Live-answer rate moves from the 55 to 75% typical of a one-counselor community to 97%+ including nights, weekends and holidays.

2. 9-field qualification without sounding like a form

The agent captures, inside a normal conversation: prospective resident name and age, caller relationship, current living situation, care level (IL, AL, MC, respite, life plan), mobility (walker, wheelchair, transfer assist), payer source (private pay, LTC insurance, VA Aid & Attendance, Medicaid waiver where accepted, bridge loan), target move-in window, zip code (drive-time check), and preferred time for a first tour. Everything writes to the CRM as a structured record.

3. Tour booking directly into the sales counselor calendar

When the lead qualifies, the agent books a tour (in-person, virtual or hybrid) on the real counselor calendar, respecting drive-time for off-site tours and the dining service schedule for in-person. No "we will call you back." A confirmation with the time, address and what to bring is sent by SMS and WhatsApp with a one-tap reschedule option.

4. Pre-tour nurture to lift show rate

Seventy-two hours out, the agent sends a short video from the executive director plus parking and front-desk instructions. Twenty-four hours out, a text with weather and dining menu. Two hours out, a reminder with the counselor's direct number. Communities that run this sequence routinely see tour show rate move from the 60 to 70% range into the mid-to-high 80s.

5. Structured 4-touch nurture across the 70 to 120 day decision window

For every unqualified or not-yet-ready lead, the agent runs a scheduled nurture: day 7 (resources on the care level the family inquired about), day 21 (invitation to a community event or caregiver support group), day 45 (second-tour or lunch-and-learn offer), day 75 and day 110 (status check with a specific ask). Each touch is on the family's preferred channel, each writes back to the CRM, and the counselor only sees the leads that reply.

6. Tour-to-move-in follow-up with real content, not platitudes

Inside 24 hours of the tour the agent sends a personalized recap built from the counselor's tour notes: the apartment the family liked, the specific concerns the daughter raised about her mother's mobility, the next decision step. Not "thanks for visiting." The McKnight's and Siro bodies of research agree that customized follow-up is the single biggest tour-to-move-in lever; the agent makes it default instead of aspirational.

7. Dormant family reactivation on a 6-month and 12-month cadence

Senior living inquiries that went cold rarely died; they got absorbed by a hospitalization, a spouse crisis or a different community that ran a tighter follow-up. At month 6 and month 12 the agent reaches out with a specific reason to reconnect (a new memory care wing, a price change on a floor plan, a new executive director). Expect to pull 6 to 14 recovered move-ins per year from a 140-unit community.

8. Out-of-scope handoff with a clean record

Clinical questions, dementia-care clinical assessment, hospital-discharge urgency, benefit-eligibility determinations, fair-housing-sensitive questions, and any request that implies a medical opinion route straight to the sales counselor or the director of health services with a structured summary. The agent never improvises on any of those. (More on the refuse-list below.)

The AI agent layer

  • Voice: Vapi or Retell as the voice stack, with ElevenLabs or Cartesia for the voice, Claude Haiku or GPT-4o-mini for the intake brain. Latency budget under 700 ms for turn-taking so an 80-year-old caller does not hang up.
  • WhatsApp: Meta Cloud API via a Business Solution Provider (360dialog, Gupshup or Twilio's WhatsApp product). Templates for the tour reminder, the pre-tour video delivery, the post-tour recap, and each nurture touch. Family members prefer WhatsApp more than the industry assumes.
  • SMS: 10DLC-registered number through Twilio or Telnyx. Brand registration first, campaign second. Expect 1-3 weeks for a clean registration in late 2026 under current Campaign Registry rules.
  • Web chat: Embedded on the community site with the same prompt and the same writeback. The handoff model should degrade gracefully to SMS if the visitor closes the tab.
  • Payments: Stripe for refundable deposits, with a pay link rather than card numbers over text.
  • Marketing attribution: UTM capture on every form, call tracking numbers from CallRail or Invoca, and source stamp on every CRM record so the quarterly spend review is traceable.

Vendor playbooks worth benchmarking against

TalkFurther, SkyPoint SkyAdvisor, Waypoint Converts, Alineops and Marchex all ship senior living specific voice and nurture products. They work. They are shallower on custom CRM writeback than a built-for-you agent and typically run $699 to $2,400 per community per month on per-lead or per-minute metering. Pick a packaged vendor when you have four or fewer communities and a standard CRM. Pick a built-for-you agent when the writeback depth matters: custom floor plan inventory, waitlist logic, bridge-loan rules, or a second-language (Spanish or Mandarin in several US metros) nurture track.

Compliance: the four rails that keep a community safe

1. HIPAA scope and the BAA

The moment a family tells the agent about a parent's dementia diagnosis, mobility level, medication list or payer, that conversation contains PHI. Treat the AI agent as a business associate. Get a signed BAA from the voice provider, the LLM provider (Anthropic's Claude for Work and OpenAI's Business tier both offer them), the BSP and the CRM. Confirm the BAA names the AI features in writing, not just the base phone or email service.

2. TCPA, CAN-SPAM and 10DLC for outbound

Inbound is the low-risk starting point. The second the agent starts dialing or texting out, TCPA consent language, written capture, honored opt-outs and 10DLC Campaign Registry registration all apply. A senior living community does not qualify for the healthcare prior-business-relationship exemption the way a hospital does, so keep consent clean. Work with counsel on the consent record; one record covering voice, SMS, WhatsApp and email is cleaner than four.

3. Fair housing and ADA language

The agent can never ask, imply or record anything that would steer a prospect based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability status, or age in a way that violates the Fair Housing Act. Senior-only communities have a 55+ exemption under HOPA, but even inside that exemption the agent cannot screen for anything else. The system prompt names this explicitly and the refuse-list below enforces it.

4. The explicit refuse-list

The agent must refuse and route for these every time:

  • Any clinical diagnosis or assessment, including whether a parent qualifies for memory care
  • Medication dosing or management guidance
  • Hospital-to-move-in urgency inside 72 hours without a sales counselor or director of health services on the line
  • Anything that reads as fair housing steering
  • Promises that Medicare will pay for IL or AL rent (it does not)
  • Statements that a specific LTC insurance policy will pay out without a benefit check
  • Price negotiation beyond published entry fee and monthly fee ranges
  • Guarantees of outcome, safety, non-falls or non-hospitalizations
  • Any statement about resident mix, staff ratios or incident history beyond the community's current published numbers

5. Voice disclosure

Several states and the FCC's 2024-2026 updates require disclosing that the caller is speaking with an AI voice. The disclosure can be as simple as "You are speaking with Grace, our AI assistant at Oakridge. A sales counselor can jump on the line any time, just say so." Put it in the opening turn, not buried in a long preamble.

2-week rollout plan

  1. Day 1-3. Discovery and data. Pull the last 180 days of calls, SMS and web form inquiries from Yardi Senior CRM, Enquire, Eldermark or whichever system runs the funnel. Map the top 10 inquiry types. Capture the current counselor's actual opening script (not the one in the sales playbook).
  2. Day 4-7. WhatsApp and web chat first. Launch the agent on WhatsApp and the community site chat widget. Agent writes new leads to the CRM with source, transcript and UTM. No voice yet. Target: 95% of site visitors get a reply inside 60 seconds.
  3. Day 8-10. Voice. Port or forward the community main number to the voice agent with a fallback to the counselor's mobile for anything the agent flags as emergent. Opening disclosure in the first turn. Keep the answering service in the loop as a backup for the first two weeks, then retire it.
  4. Day 11-13. Nurture and reactivation. Launch the 7/21/45/75/110 day nurture against the current month's pipeline. Launch the 6-month and 12-month dormant family reactivation against the last 24 months of inquiries. Both write to the CRM with structured records.
  5. Day 14. Review. Live-answer rate, first-reply time, inquiries captured, tours booked, show rate, deposits taken, move-ins committed. Kill what did not work. Expand what did.

Day 30 and day 90 reviews follow the same template. By day 90 the only honest question is whether the community can staff dining and care for the new occupancy, because inquiry response is no longer the bottleneck.

Who should not do this yet

Three cases where we tell operators to wait:

  • Under 40 inquiries per month per community. The volume does not justify the build. Use a tight voicemail script plus a $4-$6/call answering service.
  • CRM in use with no open API (older Eldermark deployments without the integration tier, or legacy ECP installations). The writeback cost kills the ROI.
  • No signed BAA available from the current CRM vendor, or no legal review of the TCPA consent flow. Do not work around either. Fix both first.
