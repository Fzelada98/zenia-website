AI Agent for Hospice Agencies: The 2026 US Playbook

Short answer: the discharge planner pages a referral at 5:47 p.m. on Friday and the median US hospice stay is 18 days (MedPAC, March 2026). Every hour from referral to admission is ~5.5% of that patient's time on service. An AI agent on voice and WhatsApp that answers in under 60 seconds, prescreens eligibility, books the RN admission visit into Axxess, Homecare Homebase, MatrixCare, WellSky or CareVoyant, and pushes status back to the referring hospital or SNF is the fastest way to move the referral-to-admission clock from a reported 24+ hour industry average toward the BerryDunn 8-hour target and the Hospice Advisors 3.5-hour high-performer mark.

Why hospice intake is the lever in 2026

Over 51% of Medicare decedents used the hospice benefit in 2023 and the share hit a new high in 2024, per MedPAC's March 2026 report. Average lifetime length of stay was 99.6 days (preliminary MedPAC, December 2025); median was 18 days. A quarter of enrollees die within five days of admission. The agency with the shortest intake clock captures the referral the hospital case manager made under pressure at shift change. The one that returns the call on Monday morning loses the patient, or worse, misses a Saturday death with no hospice support at home.

Hospice Advisors reports a one-hour first response as the industry standard, 30 minutes as top tier, and roughly 3.5 hours as the referral-to-admission average for high performers. BerryDunn recommends an 8-hour average. A Michigan hospice association presentation documents agencies averaging over 24 hours. Conversion follows speed: the Hospice Advisors benchmark pegs the gold-standard referral-to-admission rate at 85% over a 90-day rolling window and the industry standard at 75-80%. Axxess tells customers to review intake if they drop below that band. AccentCare has publicly cited a 52% rate against a sub-two-hour response. The low end is where an intake queue sitting in an inbox becomes a referral-partner relationship problem.

What the AI agent actually does (8 intake jobs)

This is not a general-purpose assistant. It is a narrow operator with eight well-defined jobs, each with explicit guardrails.

1. First-ring pickup, 24/7. Every referral path points at one number and one WhatsApp thread: hospital case management, SNF social workers, physician office intake, oncology navigators, family members, and the agency's own on-call line. The agent answers in under 60 seconds across voice and messaging, including the 5 p.m. Friday to 8 a.m. Monday window that answering services triage without clinical context.

2. Structured 11-field referral intake. Patient name, DOB, primary terminal diagnosis, secondary diagnoses, estimated prognosis, Medicare/Medicaid/commercial insurance, primary caregiver and relationship, address and geography, DNR/POLST status, referring provider and facility, urgency. The agent fills the structure, confirms the fields back verbatim, and writes them into Axxess, Homecare Homebase, MatrixCare, WellSky or CareVoyant through the agency's existing referral API or Zapier interface.

3. Medicare hospice benefit prescreen. The agent walks the referrer or family through structural eligibility questions: terminal diagnosis with a six-month prognosis if the disease runs its normal course, two-physician certification, election of the hospice benefit, forgoing curative treatment for the terminal condition. Edge cases (open chemotherapy, active clinical trial, dual-eligible with managed care, VA concurrent care) flag for the clinical intake nurse. The agent does not make the medical eligibility call. It routes the call.

4. Admission visit booking against the RN calendar and geography. The agent reads the admission RN team's calendar, the patient's ZIP code, the referral urgency, and the next available visit window that respects geography. It confirms with the family in plain language and sends a WhatsApp or SMS confirmation with the nurse's first name and the window.

5. Referral-source status push. The case manager who faxed the referral at 4:47 p.m. does not want another phone call the next morning. The agent pushes three status messages back to the referring hospital, SNF or physician office: "referral received and in intake," "RN scheduled for admission at [time]," "patient admitted to service," through the referrer's preferred channel (secure fax, portal, or encrypted email). This is the single most cited loyalty driver in hospice referral partnerships.

6. Pre-admission family comfort nurture. The window between referral and admission is where families bail. The agent sends two scripted WhatsApp messages: what to expect at the admission visit, who shows up, what the nurse will ask, what medications stay or change, what the Medicare benefit covers, who to call at 2 a.m. if symptoms worsen before admission. No medical advice. Expectation setting.

7. On-service caregiver reminders. Scheduled CNA visits, medication deliveries, chaplain and social work visits, respite week logistics, DME pickups, bereavement check-ins. The agent runs the cadence against the agency's scheduling system and raises the hand to a human when a caregiver answers "we are not doing okay."

8. Volunteer and bereavement intake. Volunteer recruiting is understaffed at most community hospices. The agent runs the first-touch intake for volunteer applicants against the agency's background check and training workflow, and handles bereaved family check-ins at the 13-month CMS-required close. Both tracks hand off to a human at the right step.

Case study: 112-ADC community hospice in Nashville

After 90 days live, referral volume moved to 51/month (two lost sources returned, two new SNF relationships opened on status-push reliability), referral-to-admit-visit to 5.1h on average (BerryDunn target beaten), 5-day-or-less stays to 24%, intake coordinator time to 11h/week (redeployed to referral-source relationship work), and volunteer applicants to 14/month.

Four compliance rails every hospice has to build around

HIPAA with a BAA scope that includes the model. Every vendor signs a BAA, and the LLM path is explicitly inside it (Anthropic enterprise, OpenAI enterprise/ZDR, Google Vertex healthcare contracts). No BAA covering the model layer, no PHI. The agent minimizes PHI at the voice layer and never recites the Medicare beneficiary identifier back on the phone.

TCPA and 10DLC. Outbound SMS and WhatsApp to patients and families runs under TCPA. There is no blanket healthcare exemption for marketing. Operational and care-coordination messages have more room under HHS/FCC "healthcare treatment" guidance, but the registration track (10DLC campaign, approved WhatsApp templates) is the same. Inbound-first is still the safest posture.

Medicare Conditions of Participation. CMS CoPs set the clinical fences: initial comprehensive assessment inside 5 calendar days of election, IDG meeting cycle, bereavement for 13 months after death. The agent does not do clinical work. It books the RN to do clinical work and tracks that the right events happened in the right windows, with human sign-off before anything clinical closes.

State licensure and mandatory reporting. Texas, California, New York and Florida each have quirks on in-state call handling, elder abuse reporting thresholds and language access. They live in the agent's state-aware policy layer, not in free-text prompts. Suspected abuse, neglect or self-harm routes to a human immediately and logs the handoff.

The refuse-list (what the agent will not do)

A hospice intake agent that tries to be helpful is one that will get you sued. The standard refuse-list: give a terminal prognosis or confirm six-month eligibility; promise Medicare coverage of a specific drug, treatment or level of care without a benefit check; discuss DNR or POLST content clinically; offer bereavement counseling beyond "we have a counselor who can call you tonight"; change medications, dosages or infusion rates; tell a family to stop or continue life-prolonging treatment; negotiate rates or room-and-board cost shares outside published policy; discuss staffing levels, incident reports or state-survey findings; steer by race, religion, national origin, disability or family structure; or handle any call with active suicidality, abuse disclosure or imminent safety risk, which escalates to the on-call clinician in the same message.

The FCC voice disclosure is in the opening turn of every outbound call and the first inbound message: "You are speaking with an automated assistant for [Hospice Name]. A nurse is available at any time."

The 2-week rollout

Week 1, WhatsApp and web first. Stand up the WhatsApp Business API number, register 10DLC, approve the first six templates (referral receipt, admission scheduled, admission complete, caregiver check-in, volunteer intake, bereavement check-in), wire the Axxess/HCHB/MatrixCare/WellSky referral intake path, load the state policy layer, dry-run with the intake coordinator in parallel.

Week 2, voice goes live, phone last. Main line and on-call line route through the voice agent with a bare-bones IVR fallback. Status-push channels to the top referral sources are tested with their actual case managers. Nurture cadence starts on the active pipeline. Day-30 review criteria are concrete: live-answer rate, first-reply median, referral-to-admit-visit time, 30-day rolling conversion.

Who should not do this yet

Three cases to be honest about: under 25 referrals per month, where the payback math is weak and a part-time intake coordinator with WhatsApp templates is enough; an EMR with a closed API and no appetite to pay for integration, where without referral-record write-back the agent is a glorified voicemail; and no BAA path with an LLM vendor compliance will approve, because the intake conversation is PHI from the first turn.

What to measure in the first 90 days

Six numbers, reviewed weekly: live-answer rate split by business and after-hours, median first-reply time, referral-to-admit-visit hours, referral-to-admission rate on 30-day and 90-day rolling windows, 5-day-or-less stay share, and Net Promoter from the top five referrers scored one-touch by WhatsApp. If four of six are not moving by day 45, the problem is the integration or the script. The agent is not the hard part. The hard part is treating the referral source like a customer with a service-level agreement.
